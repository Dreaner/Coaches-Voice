After the early days at Everton, we had an opportunity to roughly bed things down. My choice of system was to play my goalkeeper, a back four but we would play one in front of the back four and the man at the time was Lee Carsley and I'll explain in just a minute my reasons why, and four in front and one up top. It basically looked like a 4-1-4-1. I had felt at the time that I had really good centre halves. I had Alan Stubbs and David Weir. We had introduced Joseph Yobo a little bit near the time but for both Alan (Stubbs) and David (Weir), I didn't necessarily wanted them to come out of their slots, coming up the park too high so we used Lee Carsley to do that. And how we did that is we used Lee Carsley to play in front of the centre

halves here. I had made a decision that I didn't want David Weir or Alan Stubbs coming short with centre-forwards, coming short and leaving spaces for them to run into or also to take them short and spin where at that time, it could expose their lack of speed higher up the pitch. What these two centre halves were, they were brilliant as penalty box defenders. They were great in the box. They could defend. They could block. They read danger. They smelt danger. But I didn't want them getting caught on the halfway line which is why we introduced Lee Carsley to play in front of the back four which I felt gave the centre halves and the defenders the best chance of success. What we did do as a rule though, when we had the opposition, who at this time, most of the teams were playing with four defenders - if

any of the full-backs received the ball, it was simply, your player, up to him and then from there we would find the positions of bringing Lee (Carsley) across and trying to stop the centre-forward from getting the ball. Carsley would deal with it to allow Alan Stubbs or David Weir to stay in their slots and not get dragged out. So that was how we basically set up. We also found that if the centre halves had taken the ball, and got on the ball, the first and initial press would have come from the one centre-forward - at this time, it might have been Kevin Campbell, Tomasz Radzinski - although he may have gone by then - maybe Wayne Rooney, Duncan Ferguson and we would use any other midfielder who was to join the centre-forward to put pressure on the centre halves. What you do need, if you have a pressing team, is you need quick defenders and at this time, in this period at Everton, we didn't have the quickest central defenders - we had good central defenders - and I had to accommodate that by introducing a player to play in front of them to help them out.

As we evolved at Everton, we were looking to improve the level of player but also the type of player we were bringing into the club.
